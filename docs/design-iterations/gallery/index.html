Four directions for Case Desk

Each iteration pairs the developer console with the subscriber help chat as one design system. All four run in the real app on the same data and the same logic; only the markup, styles and view layer differ, and every one passes the end-to-end test.

Orchestrator’s pick for the demo: Board (v4). Ilijan’s favourite: Night Shift (v2), with open questions in docs/design-iterations/HANDOFF-BAGLEY.md. The current design is at the end for comparison.

Board

Orchestrator’s pick

Triage first. No sidebar: the top of the screen is a board of lanes sorted by what each case needs, and the selected case opens as a full-width spread below it.

A judge reads it in one look: 1 needs your OK, 2 need a person, 1 privacy, 14 answered. The approve button is the first thing in the first lane; on approve, Ava's ticket travels to Answered and the numerals tick. The spread sets the status sentence at 40px beside the conversation, with the checked verdict and what Case Desk did above the fold.

Swiss and typographic: Geologica on white, true black for headlines, Lumen's own coral as the only signal for “needs you” and Lumen's blue for the subscriber's voice. It holds up on a projector and is the furthest from generic SaaS.

Help leads with the account state as a headline, shows the facts as large figures, and draws a grant request as a three-step tracker (Requested, The team reviews, Given) that completes when the team approves.

Night Shift

Ilijan’s favourite

The pitch deck's control room after dark. A dense three-pane instrument in the deck's navy, with its colour grammar: amber needs you, teal is checked, coral is blocked.

It is the direction where the facts, the actions policy chose and the reply the checker passed sit on one screen, which is exactly the sentence the demo script says. Every pending grant lives in a decision dock along the bottom; its amber button is the brightest thing on screen, and the dock is gone when nothing waits.

It flows straight out of the slides: the same navy, IBM Plex and colour meanings, so the jump from deck to live demo feels like one product. Day shift (light) is designed too, for a bright room.

Help is Lumen at night: a compact status band, replies on an amber rule, the phone over a still from the deck. j and k move through cases in the console.

Statement

The account as a statement. Every case reads on one dated spine: what the store did, when the user wrote, what Case Desk did, what waits for you.

It regroups the case by time instead of by panel, which is how a developer reasons about a billing complaint: bought 12 Oct, charged 15 Oct, renewal failed 22 Oct, Ava wrote 8 Nov. The status sentence, set large in Source Serif 4, is the one bold thing on the page.

It shows the pitch's central claim as layout: the spine's facts come from Adapty, the conversation sits beside them, and the user's claims sit on the FactSheet rows that confirm or contradict them. Approving stamps the grant slip, which then folds away.

Help is a printed receipt: plan, raw price and key dates on a mini spine, Case Desk's replies as serif text on paper, and the grant as a slip that receives a dated stamp.

Correspondence

Support as correspondence. A calm mail client in the lineage of Things and Front: big humane type, a lot of air, each case read like a letter.

The most legible of the four and the friendliest for a developer who is not deep in billing: a folder rail with the four filters, an index with “Needs your OK” pinned on top, and a wide reading pane. Details unfold in place only when asked.

Each letter opens with the correspondent's name in Commissioner's flared display weight, the one memorable element, then a plain sentence: writes in Serbian, bought on the App Store on 22 Sept for 36.99 EUR. Once a grant is approved, the outcome appears right under the status.

Help is the gentlest: the status folded into one sentence under the header that unfolds to every fact, 17px text, and suggestions that wrap instead of scrolling.
